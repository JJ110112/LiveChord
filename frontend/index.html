Music-tech details

Built for musicians who listen for structure

LiveChord combines chord recognition, beat/bar alignment, melody extraction, arranger patterns, and synced instrument views so the screen follows how the music is actually played.

Chord recognition

A transformer-based pipeline turns audio into a chord timeline, then cleans short fragments so the result is readable for practice.

Beat and bar alignment

Beat tracking and bar correction keep chord cards, dots, loops, and practice views locked to the musical pulse.

Melody extraction

Melody-aware views help separate accompaniment from lead lines, useful when studying piano, guitar, or arranger-style parts.

Arranger patterns

Backing styles translate the same harmony into playable rhythm patterns instead of leaving you with static chord names.

Synced instrument views

Piano, guitar, ukulele, accordion, and arranger views share the same timing data, so fingering, voicing, and playback stay together.

How LiveChord works

  1. 1

    Upload your audio

    Drag an audio file onto the upload area or click to pick one. MP3, FLAC, WAV and OGG up to 100 MB. The file lives in memory only for the duration of analysis — it never goes into a long-term archive.

  2. 2

    AI analyzes the uploaded audio

    A chord-recognition transformer detects the chord progression. A beat-tracking model finds bar lines and tempo. A section detector identifies structural sections (e.g., verse/chorus patterns). Typical analysis takes 30 seconds to 2 minutes depending on track length.

  3. 3

    Practice with synced views

    The player opens with chords, fingering and timing in lockstep with the audio. Transpose to your singing key, slow playback to 0.5×, loop a single section A-B, switch instrument view at any time. Built for actual practice — not just casual listening.

Built in the open

LiveChord is one developer's personal practice tool, polished into a public service in 2026. The core components — including chord recognition, beat tracking, and the player UI — are open source.
